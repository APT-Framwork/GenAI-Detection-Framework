\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) represent one of the most sophisticated
and dangerous classes of cyberattacks, characterized by stealth, persistence,
and coordinated multi-stage progression. This report presents a
GenAI-Augmented Framework for Early Detection of Multi-Stage APT Attacks
using the Cyber Kill Chain. The proposed framework integrates four deep
learning models --- Autoencoder, Variational Autoencoder, Transformer, and
Graph Neural Network --- with a Reinforcement Learning agent for multi-model
fusion. SecBERT embeddings are used to exploit the semantic content of
security logs, while REaLTabFormer generates synthetic novel APT sequences
to improve model robustness. The framework is integrated into a Cyber Threat
Intelligence Platform connected to Wazuh, and validated through end-to-end
simulation using MITRE Caldera and Docker. Evaluation results demonstrate
84.1\% accuracy, 0.842 F1-score, 16.9\% FPR, and 81\% pre-detection rate.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Advanced Persistent Threat, Cyber Kill Chain,
MITRE ATT\&CK, Deep Learning, Generative AI, Graph Neural Network,
Reinforcement Learning, Intrusion Detection.

Les menaces persistantes avancées (APT) représentent l'une des classes
d'attaques informatiques les plus sophistiquées, caractérisées par leur

profond --- Autoencoder, Autoencoder variationnel, Transformer et Réseau

Caldera et Docker. Les résultats montrent une précision de 84,1\%,
un F1-score de 0,842, un taux de faux positifs de 16,9\% et un taux
de pré-détection de 81\%.

\section*{General Context and Motivation}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The rapid expansion of digital infrastructure has significantly broadened the attack
surface available to malicious actors, giving rise to increasingly sophisticated threats.
Among these, \textbf{Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)} represent the most dangerous
class of cyberattacks --- orchestrated by skilled, well-resourced adversaries such as
nation-states or organized criminal groups, with clearly defined strategic objectives
. Unlike conventional attacks, APTs are characterized by
their \textbf{persistence} and \textbf{stealth}: they infiltrate target environments and
remain undetected for extended periods, slowly advancing through a coordinated series
of interconnected stages toward their goals .

This multi-stage nature is precisely what makes APTs so difficult to detect. Traditional
security mechanisms --- firewalls, signature-based antivirus, and rule-based intrusion
detection systems --- are fundamentally reactive and largely ineffective against novel or
evolving APT techniques. While machine learning (ML) has emerged as a promising
complement, existing solutions typically treat security events in isolation, failing to
model the sequential attack progression that defines APT behavior, and are rarely
integrated into deployable, operational platforms 

Two complementary frameworks guide the structured analysis of APT behavior: the
\textbf{Cyber Kill Chain (CKC)}, which breaks an attack into seven canonical phases
(Reconnaissance, Weaponization, Delivery, Exploitation, Installation, Command \&
Control, and Actions on Objectives) , and the
\textbf{MITRE ATT\&CK framework}, which provides a granular taxonomy of adversary
Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) . Together, they
provide the structural foundation for detecting \textit{where} an attack currently resides
and disrupting it before it reaches its final objectives.

This report addresses these challenges by proposing a
\textbf{GenAI-Augmented Framework for Early Detection of Multi-Stage APT Attacks
using the Cyber Kill Chain} --- combining deep learning, generative data augmentation,
reinforcement learning, and SIEM integration into a complete, deployable solution.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Problem Statement}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Despite progress in ML-based intrusion detection, APT detection remains an open
challenge along four dimensions. \textbf{First}, most existing solutions treat security
events as independent observations, ignoring the multi-stage sequential structure that
defines APT campaigns. \textbf{Second}, models trained on limited historical datasets
fail to generalize to novel APT sequences, as real-world actors continuously evolve
their TTPs to evade detection. \textbf{Third}, the rich semantic content of security
logs --- command lines, process names, and log messages --- is largely underexploited
by approaches that rely solely on structured numerical features. \textbf{Fourth}, the gap
between research prototypes and operational deployment is rarely bridged: high-performing
models seldom integrate into real-time platforms connected to live SIEM systems.

This report directly addresses all four dimensions through an end-to-end framework that
models attack sequences across the CKC, augments training data with GenAI-generated
novel sequences, leverages SecBERT embeddings for semantic log understanding, and
deploys the solution within an operational threat intelligence platform.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Research Objectives}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The main goal of this project is to ensure \textbf{early detection of APTs} by monitoring
their behavior across the Cyber Kill Chain before they reach their objectives .
The specific objectives are:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Early Multi-Stage APT Detection:}
    Develop a GenAI-augmented framework  to detect APT activity as early
    as possible in the attack lifecycle.

    \item \textbf{CKC/ATT\&CK Mapping:}
    Build a unified mapping between the Cyber Kill Chain and MITRE ATT\&CK to
    represent attacks in a structured, stage-based way.

    \item \textbf{GenAI for Robustness:}
    Use REaLTabFormer to generate novel APT sequences and SecBERT to enrich
    textual log features, improving robustness and generalization.

    \item \textbf{Developed CTI Platform:}
    Develop a Cyber Threat Intelligence Platform connected to \textbf{SIEM}, and
    validate it through simulation with MITRE Caldera and Docker.

\end{itemize}

\section*{Report Organization}

The remainder of this report is structured as follows.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:background}} establishes the theoretical foundations.
It covers cybersecurity principles, the APT lifecycle, the Cyber Kill Chain and MITRE
ATT\&CK frameworks, machine learning paradigms, Generative AI, and the five model
architectures used in this work.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:related}} surveys APT detection solutions in the literature.
It reviews available datasets and identifies the gaps addressed by this report.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:conception}} details the full framework design.
It presents dataset selection, preprocessing, feature engineering, model architectures,
synthetic data generation, and the RL agent.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}} describes the implementation environment.
It includes dataset statistics and the Cyber Threat Intelligence Platform with Wazuh
integration.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}} presents the evaluation results.
It reports per-model metrics, the ablation study, the impact of synthetic data, and the
end-to-end simulation results.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}} summarizes the main contributions.
It also discusses the limitations of the work and outlines future research directions.
